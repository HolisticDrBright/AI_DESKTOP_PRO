# One-login clinic-approved workforce access — decision and release contract

Owner decision (15 October 2026): a practitioner may create one Desktop account and request to join a clinic or propose a new clinic. A clinic-approved membership, not the account or a self-supplied token attribute, grants clinical access. Creating a clinic also requires review. Account creation and email verification do not approve a membership. This is the selected successor to the current immutable-Cognito-attribute model; it does **not** activate the successor.

## Observed baseline and non-negotiable boundary

- Production workforce pool `us-east-2_AZMa5gxSZ` has immutable `custom:person_id`, `custom:organization_id` and `custom:production_bound` fields. The public clients permit only `email` writes. Direct Cognito self-sign-up remains enabled at the owner's request; the app's own registration route is still compile-time disabled by `WORKFORCE_SELF_SIGNUP_READY = false`.
- The production compatibility API takes person and organization from those three JWT claims. The compiled production schema's `set_request_context` validates the identity/person and the individual clinical functions commonly check current membership. That is not a universal per-request membership proof: `assert_production_context` checks context values, not membership, and the unregistered token trigger cannot revoke a token already issued. No general claim that removal fences every workforce endpoint is supported yet.
- The source-only pre-token handler and private lookup are **blocked candidates**, not a Cognito trigger, Lambda or applied migration. They can help mint initial legacy claims for one approved clinic but cannot be the sole authority. They currently refuse more than one active clinic. Keep those refusals until selected-clinic session binding is designed and tested.
- No PHI, production activation, paid mobile build, Cognito trigger change, AWS schema write, or release flag change is authorized by this choice. Preserve the current production pilot boundary and synthetic-only qualification posture.

## Chosen end state

1. Cognito proves the person's login subject and verified email. No applicant-supplied clinic, role, person ID, or production flag is accepted as authorization. A trusted onboarding service binds the immutable Cognito `sub` to a database person without clinical access, using a dedicated narrow role and an audited, idempotent operation. Duplicate subjects, duplicate email claims, subject swaps, disabled identities and retry races fail closed. An unapproved account can read only its own application status and may submit a bounded join or clinic-creation request; it cannot use patient, chart, message, program, billing, export, or provider routes.
2. A join request names an exact existing clinic without revealing its patient or staff directory. Only an active owner/admin for that clinic may approve or reject it. A new-clinic proposal is approved by a separately authorized platform operator; the applicant cannot create an active organization or make themself its owner. Every approval, rejection and revocation is reasoned and audited. The membership starts in a non-clinical state and becomes active only through the approval transaction.
3. Clinical requests resolve the subject to an active person, an active **selected** clinic and current active membership in the authoritative database on every operation. The selected clinic is bound to the request/session; it is not a mutable global preference. A token for clinic A cannot select B without a current B membership. Two devices may select different approved clinics concurrently. Role changes and removals must fence old tokens, not merely wait for token refresh. Database or directory outage refuses clinical access.
4. Maintain the old pilot token contract until every workforce entry point uses the database authority: production compatibility and its fallback, identity/consent, telehealth, recording/voice, Ask ALP, governed catalog, lab analysis, programs/media, Fullscript and any newly added route. Consumer identity is a separate trust domain and must not be changed as a shortcut. No endpoint may derive authorization solely from `custom:organization_id` after the successor activates.
5. A compatibility period may carry derived legacy claims for already-bound practitioners, but they are hints for routing only; database-currentness remains mandatory. A missing binding or database outage must not silently fall back to Cognito attributes. Existing practitioners require a backfill/shadow comparison and rollback drill before a trigger is attached to their live pool.

## Release acceptance — all required, fictional identities first

- Fresh sign-up, email confirmation and mandatory MFA; no clinical claims or patient access before approval, even with a direct Cognito sign-up bypassing the Desktop form.
- Exact-clinic join request, admin approval, rejection, replay, concurrent approvals, self-approval refusal, cross-clinic administrator refusal, clinic-creation proposal/operator review, and idempotent lost replies.
- One clinic approved: only that clinic's rows and actions; unrelated clinic and consumer tokens refused. Two clinics approved: explicit per-session selection, two simultaneous devices, no cross-device selection bleed.
- Removal, role demotion, person/identity disable, organization suspension and database outage: an already-issued JWT is refused on **every** clinical route. Race the revocation with an in-flight read/write, not only with the next sign-in.
- No raw email or sensitive application details in audit/logs; bounded requests and responses, shared edge throttling, abuse monitoring and support recovery. Test duplicate email and account-switch behavior.
- Exact source/SQL/template hashes, rollback and preservation checks, local database and handler negatives, green exact-head CI, then hosted qualification against fictional accounts and real AWS services. Human security/retention/provider reviews and physical-device acceptance are separate gates. Only afterward may a reviewed production change set and release decision be considered.

## Immediate engineering sequence

1. Build a checked inventory of every workforce token-claim consumer and classify whether it verifies current database membership inside the same operation. The list above is a starting point, not an exhaustive certification.
2. Design and test the narrow onboarding/application ledger and approval transactions with separate applicant, clinic-admin and platform-operator authorities. Do not reuse the clinical API role for unapproved applicants.
3. Build one shared request-time membership resolution contract and migrate the workforce entry points behind a blocked successor. Specifically audit the production compatibility fallback and the `activate_my_memberships` pending-invitation path before tightening the request-context function, so existing practitioners are not locked out by accident.
4. Qualify multi-clinic session selection and revocation races, then a preserving existing-account backfill and shadow run. The current source-only pre-token candidate is not a complete substitute.
5. Only after the complete route matrix passes may the self-sign-up release constant, Cognito trigger and any production pool configuration be proposed for activation. Any AWS execution needs a separate exact change-set review. PHI remains disabled until the wider clinical, security and agreement gates close.

## Source candidate after the decision

`infra/aws-clinical-core/source-candidates/workforce-clinic-approved-onboarding.sql` is an **unregistered** successor, applied only to the assembled production schema in an embedded fictional database test. It separates a non-login applicant-service role, the existing clinical API role for current clinic-admin review, and a non-login platform-operator role for clinic creation. Applicant data is an email digest and Cognito subject, not a raw email. Direct table access is revoked. A partial unique index allows at most one pending application per subject; short advisory subject locks and row locks settle retries. An append-only decision ledger records who reviewed each request. The approved transaction creates the bound person/identity/directory and active membership together. A suspended owner/admin reapplying as a practitioner cannot regain the old elevated role.

`src/server/clinical-core/aws-workforce-applications-api.ts` is a **source-only** applicant boundary. Its release constant is blocked and nothing imports it as a deployed Lambda. In a fictional qualification configuration it accepts only the isolated workforce pool/client/account, checks the current Cognito identity rather than only a stale token, derives the email digest server-side, and exposes only submit/status/withdraw for the authenticated subject. No applicant-supplied clinical identifier is accepted. Its database connection must use only the narrow applicant role; no such deployed connection exists yet.

`infra/aws-clinical-core/source-candidates/workforce-current-membership-context.sql` is a second **unregistered** candidate. It resolves an exact selected clinic and person from active database rows using only the signed-in subject, locks those rows for the enclosing operation, and refuses an unapproved, removed, disabled, foreign-clinic or non-clinical `member` subject. Its fictional database test covers two concurrently selectable clinics without a global current-clinic setting. A new `database_current` branch in the production compatibility adapter **and the deployed identity Lambda's source handler** uses that setter and performs its operation in the same transaction. The source handler refuses other workforce routes in that mode rather than falling back to legacy claims. The mode is not selected by current Lambda configuration, the SQL is not registered, the legacy pilot path remains unchanged, and the production PHI gate still refuses while disabled. The successor is still limited to the existing pilot operation and clinic policy, not the general multi-clinic commercial release.

The local candidate tests cover the role split, preapproval refusal, clinic and platform review, idempotent retries, rejection/withdrawal, current-membership token lookup, suspended-admin reapproval, selected-clinic isolation and same-transaction compatibility calls. They do **not** prove real Cognito behavior, hosted PostgreSQL privileges or lock races, actor binding for a platform operator, distributed throttling, approval-route integration, the other workforce Lambda routes, two physical devices or immediate old-token revocation across the whole app. The status remains **design selected; source candidate locally verified; hosted/device acceptance and activation open**. Do not describe practitioner self-sign-up as operational clinical onboarding yet.
